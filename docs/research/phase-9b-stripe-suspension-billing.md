# Stripe billing semantics during Temporary Suspension

Researched 2026-09-21 UTC from public primary sources for [Research Stripe billing semantics during Temporary Suspension](https://github.com/nick-neely/tendnote/issues/583). It resolves the open item left by [Bounded usage and the author-operated support contract](../phase-9b/bounded-usage-and-support-contract.md): what happens to invoices, prepaid days, and the period anchor while a Temporary Suspension is open. All sources are Stripe's own documentation, accessed 2026-09-21. No Stripe account, configuration, or API was touched.

## Two different Stripe features, and only one of them matches suspension

Stripe documents two things that are both called pausing, with opposite intents.

**Pause payment collection** keeps the service and stops the money. "When you pause payment collection, the subscription remains `active` and invoices continue to generate, but Stripe doesn't collect payment. Your customer retains access to the service during this time." Stripe names the intent explicitly: "to temporarily offer a customer your services for free". [Pause payment collection](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/pause-payment)

That is the inverse of Temporary Suspension, which stops the service and has no reason to stop the money. The bounded-usage draft already recorded that pausing collection "still generates invoices and leaves subscription status unchanged"; that is confirmed verbatim. The same page states the subscription's "status remains unchanged" during the pause and that "Invoices created before you pause subscriptions continue to be retried unless you void them", so it does not even stop an in-flight dunning cycle.

The three invoice behaviours, if it were ever used: `keep_as_draft` creates invoices "in `draft` status with `auto_advance` set to false"; `mark_uncollectible` marks them uncollectible, but "Stripe applies any existing customer balance to invoices" first and the invoice becomes `paid` if the balance covers it; `void` voids them, with "No record of charges is created for these periods". Resuming is unsetting `pause_collection`, and draft invoices are not collected automatically: you must set `auto_advance=true` on each one afterwards. `resumes_at` is an optional Unix timestamp for automatic resumption. A schedule does not clear it: "A subscription schedule phase transition doesn't automatically remove `pause_collection` behavior that was set directly on the subscription." [Pause payment collection](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/pause-payment)

**Pause subscriptions** is the feature whose semantics match: "Pause a subscription to temporarily suspend service delivery and invoice generation without canceling it... It also gives you a paused state for retention flows, support tooling, and testing billing, entitlement, and webhook behavior." After pausing: "The subscription status changes to `paused`", "The `current_period_end` updates to the time you paused the subscription", "Stripe pauses invoice generation until you resume the subscription", and Stripe emits `customer.subscription.paused`, `customer.subscription.updated`, and `entitlements.active_entitlement_summary.updated`. [Pause subscriptions](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/pause)

It also answers the prepaid-days question directly, inside the pause call. `bill_for[unused_time_from]` defaults to `now`, which credits "unused time from when you pause the subscription through the end of the period"; `none` declines to credit it. `invoicing_behavior` defaults to `pending_invoice_item`, which parks the credit until the next invoice rather than invoicing it immediately. Stripe's worked example: pause on day 15 of a 30 USD monthly subscription creates "a -15 USD pending invoice item (credit for 15 unused days)", and resuming on day 45 with `billing_cycle_anchor=now` produces a resumption invoice carrying the -15 USD credit and a 30 USD charge, netting 15 USD, after which "a new 30-day billing cycle begins". [Pause subscriptions](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/pause)

**The cost is that this is not generally available.** "Subscriptions must use flexible billing mode, and the pause subscription endpoint requires API version `2025-06-30.preview` or later." It also refuses several states Tendnote can reach: `send_invoice` collection, classic billing mode, `trialing`, `unpaid`, `incomplete`, and "Has an attached schedule", which must be released first. [Pause subscriptions](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/pause)

Neither feature is customer-operable. "The customer portal shows that the subscription is paused, but subscribers can't use the portal to pause subscriptions themselves" ([Pause subscriptions](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/pause)), and the portal configuration surface has no pause option at all: it covers plan switching, quantities, prorations, downgrades, promotion codes, cancellation, retention coupons, billing information, payment methods, and invoice history ([Configure the customer portal](https://docs.stripe.com/customer-management/configure-portal)). That is consistent with Temporary Suspension, which is an Operator Action and must not be reachable from the restricted area's billing link.

## What resumption does to the period anchor

Resuming a `paused` subscription is `POST /v1/subscriptions/:id/resume`, and it resets the anchor by default. `billing_cycle_anchor` is "Either `now` or `unchanged`. The default is `now`", where `now` means "Reset the subscription's billing cycle anchor to the current time (in UTC) and start a new billing period" and `unchanged` means "Advance the subscription to the period that surrounds the current time without resetting the billing cycle anchor". Prorations only arise from `unchanged`: "When the `billing_cycle_anchor` is set to `now` (default value), no prorations are generated. If no value is passed, the default is `create_prorations`." [Resume a subscription](https://docs.stripe.com/api/subscriptions/resume)

Resumption is not guaranteed to succeed, and its failure mode is quiet. "Resume is only available for subscriptions that use `charge_automatically` collection... If the invoice is manually voided, the subscription stays `paused`. If there is no payment attempt within 23 hours, Stripe voids the invoice and the subscription stays `paused`." [Resume a subscription](https://docs.stripe.com/api/subscriptions/resume) The guide is blunter: "If your billing settings automatically void failed invoices, a failed resumption invoice can leave the subscription in `paused` status with no error returned. The resume request returns `200` and the subscription stays `paused`." The `payment_behavior` parameter picks which way it fails: `resume_on_payment_attempt` (default) leaves a failed subscription `past_due` and "doesn't automatically revert to `paused`", while `resume_on_payment_success` leaves it `paused` and keeps retrying. [Pause subscriptions](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/pause)

**Design inference.** An anchor reset is not free for Tendnote even though it is free for Stripe. The bounded-usage decision displays "Resets on <date>" from the Stripe billing period and resets the Fair-Use Budget and Account Ceiling on it. Resetting the anchor on lift therefore hands the customer a fresh monthly allowance at an arbitrary date chosen by the operator, and a suspension of a few days silently re-dates every usage notice. `billing_cycle_anchor=unchanged` preserves the displayed date but reintroduces prorations. Leaving the subscription alone avoids the choice.

## Crediting already-paid days

A credit note is the documented instrument for adjusting a finalized invoice. "Credit notes are documents that decrease the amount of an `open` or `paid` invoice", and unlike a revision "a credit note doesn't void and replace the original invoice". It works on `open`, `paid`, or `uncollectible` invoices. [Issue credit notes](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/dashboard/credit-notes), [Generate credit notes programmatically](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/integration/programmatic-credit-notes)

For a `paid` invoice the amount due does not change; the money moves one of four ways, and the split must be exact: "For a `paid` invoice, the sum of the refund, credit, and out-of-band payment amounts must equal the credit note total." Those are `refund_amount` (refund to the original payment method), a linked existing refund, `credit_amount` (added to the customer credit balance, "which is automatically applied to their future invoices"), and `out_of_band_amount` (credited outside Stripe). A partial credit is expressible per line item, either as an amount or as a quantity, and Stripe recommends it: "We recommend discounting invoice line items when possible, since it associates each credit with a line item. Adding a custom discount line item can make reporting and tracking difficult."

Two constraints bear directly on a Stripe Tax account. First, **custom line items are unavailable**: "You can't add a custom line item to a credit note when automatic tax is enabled. Custom line items aren't tied to a specific invoice line item, so when the original invoice has line items with different tax treatments, Stripe can't determine which tax treatment to apply. To credit an amount from the original invoice, use an invoice line item instead." Second, credit notes sit at the end of the invoice arithmetic: "On an invoice, credit notes appear as items after applying discounts and taxes, making them a post-tax adjusted amount", with the documented order being line items, discounts, pre-tax total, taxes, customer credit balance, credit notes applied, new amount due. [Generate credit notes programmatically](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/integration/programmatic-credit-notes)

Also documented, and easy to trip over: a credit note against an invoice that already consumed a customer credit balance splits the money. "if a credit balance of 150 USD is applied to an invoice for 200 USD, then the finalized invoice is for 50 USD. If you issue a credit note for 50 USD or less, the funds are refunded to the customer's payment method. Anything above 50 USD is added to the customer's credit balance." Percentage and fixed discounts spread proportionally across line items, so a line-item credit returns the discounted amount, not the list amount. Voiding a credit note is possible "only if it's on an open invoice". [Issue credit notes](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/dashboard/credit-notes)

## Stripe Tax on the adjustment

This is the one question with an unambiguous published answer. Stripe Tax lists exactly which operations move the reported tax balance. Decreases come from "Voiding an invoice", "Marking an invoice as uncollectible", "Creating a credit note", "Creating a refund of a charge associated with an invoice or a Checkout Session", and a Tax API reversal. Increases come from invoice finalization, a completed Checkout payment, `uncollectible` to `paid`, "Voiding a credit note", and a Tax API transaction. [Tax reporting](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/reports)

So a plain `/v1/refunds` refund against an invoice charge is **not** tax-blind: it decreases reported tax by itself. The credit note is still the better instrument, because it is the one that records what was credited and against which line item, and because the itemized export carries `type=credit_note` and `transaction_type=reversal` with `reversal_original_tax_transaction_id`. A bare refund gives the reversal without the line-item story.

Three caveats from the same page. Tax is fixed at finalization: "We consider transactions effective on the date they finalize and we don't recalculate taxes afterwards." Upheld disputes never reduce it. And partial tax manipulation is explicitly discouraged: "If you refund a tax amount such that the total tax is no longer proportional to the subtotal, your tax reporting can be unreliable... We recommend not refunding partial line item tax amounts. Instead, fully reverse the transaction and create a new one with appropriate inputs for an accurate tax calculation." US location reports carry a further warning: "Stripe Tax currently doesn't support use cases beyond your transaction data, such as credits, prepayments, discounts", and refunds are reported back in the original transaction's period, "even if the refund occurred much later", which cannot be reassigned. [Tax reporting](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/reports)

**Inference, not documented.** Crediting a proportional amount of an `invoice_line_item` on an automatic-tax invoice should reverse that line's tax in the same proportion, since Stripe blocks custom line items precisely because they lack a line's tax treatment and since the reversal is recorded against the original tax transaction. Stripe does not state the proportional-tax rule for credit notes in so many words. Verify once in a sandbox with Stripe Tax enabled before relying on it.

## Recommended policy

**Do nothing to Stripe during a Temporary Suspension.** Do not pause collection, do not pause the subscription, do not attach a schedule, do not extend a trial. The subscription stays `active`, invoices finalize and are paid on the existing anchor, the period keeps advancing, and Paid Access stays true in the Stripe projection. Admission is denied by the suspension record, which is already the authority: ADR 0248 has each blocking condition owning its own exceptions, Suspension has no admission exception, and "Direct edits to the Stripe projection are prohibited; every operator change is a record the projection reads". Suspension is a review state with a ten business day internal deadline, not a billing state, and nothing in Stripe needs to know about it.

**Compensate prepaid days once, on the audited transition that ends the review, not while it is open.**

- *Lift.* Issue one credit note against the most recently paid invoice for that subscription, crediting the subscription's `invoice_line_item` by amount, at suspended days divided by period days, rounded down to the cent, with the whole amount as `credit_amount`. Stripe Tax reverses in proportion to the credited line, the credit lands on the customer credit balance, and the next renewal invoice consumes it automatically. No refund, no anchor change, no proration, no resumption invoice, and the displayed "Resets on <date>" stays true throughout.
- *Termination.* Same credit note, but with the amount as `refund_amount` so the money leaves rather than sitting on a balance no future invoice will consume. Whether the remainder is refunded at all remains the counsel item on the Hosted Obligations Register; this fixes only the mechanism if the answer is yes.
- *Suspension shorter than one day, or an amount below one cent.* Credit nothing and record why.

**Resumption does nothing**, because nothing was suspended in Stripe. The anchor is never touched, so `billing_cycle_anchor` and `proration_behavior` never come up, and the silent `paused` resume failure cannot occur.

**Revisit only if suspensions become long.** If a review routinely runs past one billing period, the honest instrument is the GA pause-subscriptions feature once it leaves preview, with `bill_for[unused_time_from]=now` and `billing_cycle_anchor=unchanged` on resume. At the current scale, one credit note per suspension by hand is less machinery than a preview API version, flexible billing mode, and a resume path that can return 200 and leave the customer paused.

## Failure cases each option carries

| Option | Failure case |
| --- | --- |
| Do nothing plus a credit note on exit (recommended) | The customer is billed a full renewal mid-review and sees a charge for a product they cannot reach. The credit arrives later, on exit, not at the moment of the charge. A customer who cancels during the suspension never receives another invoice, so a `credit_amount` credit is stranded; that case must use `refund_amount`. Renewal can also fail mid-suspension, starting a seven-day dunning window against an account nobody is using. |
| `pause_collection` | Semantically backwards: service continues in Stripe's model while Tendnote denies it, and the subscription stays `active`. With `keep_as_draft` the operator must set `auto_advance=true` on every draft afterwards or revenue silently stops. With `void` there is no record of the period at all. With `mark_uncollectible` an existing customer balance is spent first. It does not compensate prepaid days, does not stop an in-flight dunning retry, and survives a schedule phase transition. |
| Pause subscriptions (`/pause`) | Preview API version, flexible billing mode, `charge_automatically` only, refuses `trialing`, `unpaid`, `incomplete`, or any attached schedule. `current_period_end` is truncated to the pause time, which breaks the displayed budget reset date. Resume can return 200 and leave the subscription `paused` with no error, or leave it `past_due` depending on `payment_behavior`. Pinning a preview version for one Operator Action pulls the whole integration onto it. |
| Prorated refund via `/v1/refunds` alone | It does decrease reported tax, so it is not a tax bug, but it records no line-item reason and no credit note document. Partial tax handling is explicitly discouraged by Stripe. The refunded amount also lands back in the original transaction's period in US location reports and cannot be reassigned. Tendnote already revokes Paid Access on any refund, so a partial refund risks colliding with the refund-revocation rule unless that rule is narrowed to full refunds. |
| Subscription schedule with a zero-amount phase | Most machinery for least benefit: an attached schedule blocks the pause endpoint, complicates portal cancellation ("scheduled subscription updates restrict cancellation", [Portal capabilities](https://docs.stripe.com/customer-management)), and must be released afterwards. It still does not refund days already paid. |
| Extending `trial_end` | Tendnote has no trial state by decision (ADR 0226 and the lifecycle artifact), a `trialing` subscription cannot be paused later, and a trial that ends without a payment method moves the subscription to `paused` by itself. It also gives away future days rather than compensating past ones. |

## Limits and later verification

No Stripe API call, dashboard change, or payment was made; every statement above is Stripe's published documentation as of 2026-09-21, not an observation of Tendnote's account. Tendnote's billing mode (classic or flexible) has not been checked, and it decides whether the pause endpoint is even reachable. Pin the API version before implementing, and note that the pause and resume-preview endpoints cited here are documented against preview versions `2025-06-30.preview` and later.

Verify in a sandbox with Stripe Tax enabled, using test clocks, before the policy is relied on: that a partial `invoice_line_item` credit note reverses tax proportionally and appears in the itemized export as `type=credit_note`, `transaction_type=reversal`; that `credit_amount` is consumed by the next renewal invoice ahead of the card; that the credit note amount matches the discounted line amount when a coupon is present; and that a renewal failure during an open suspension interacts correctly with the seven-day dunning window.

**Still product choices:** whether a suspension that ends in Termination refunds the remainder at all (counsel, Hosted Obligations Register); whether a renewal that falls inside an open suspension should be allowed to run dunning normally or be held; whether the customer is told about the credit at suspension time or only at lift; and whether the refund-revokes-Paid-Access rule is narrowed to full refunds so a partial suspension credit does not trigger it.
