# Household Responsibility Is A Named Holder, Not An Assignment

Phase Eight lets a household-native Action or Routine name at most one active member as its Responsibility Holder, narrowly superseding ADR 0154's deferral of responsibility for household-native records only. The holder is a member's statement about who is looking after the record, never the product's claim about whose turn it is: Tendnote stores no turn order, never advances a name on completion or skip, and never names, changes, or suggests a holder from who usually does the work. That refusal is the point — an automatic rotation would assert a fact about the past the product cannot observe, since it cannot see a partner quietly covering, work done outside Tendnote, or a swap the members agreed between themselves, and a wrong assertion of that kind becomes durable timestamped evidence in a disagreement. Alternation stays seamless through an explicit one-tap hand-off offered at completion rather than through stored sequence, and a settled chore is named once and never touched again. The holder never gates authority, produces no per-member backlog or default filter, and accumulates no completion tallies, streaks, missed-turn state, or fairness score, so ADR 0165 stands intact. A named holder sends no push by itself: it may offer that member their own Reminder Schedule once, which they accept or decline, preserving ADR 0203's rule that no member's action enrolls another member's device. This accepts that a household which forgets to hand off will leave one name in place, in exchange for a product that never manufactures a fairness claim it did not witness.
